Jeevan Jhola — I built a company, then shut it down.

Reusable bags you take from anywhere and return anywhere. I recruited and led the team, planned the rollout, ran the day-to-day operations, and tested it with real users in the market.

Led: a team of interns  ·  Ran: a real market pilot  ·  Gathered: direct user feedback

What I handled: recruiting and coordinating the team, prioritising what to build first, communicating the plan, and making the calls — including the hard one.

Reality's answer: people agreed it was a good idea, then kept using whatever was already in their hand. The blocker was human habit, not the product. So I made the decision to stop — not because it was hard, but because it wasn't working. Building was the easy part. Deciding was the hard part.

Working principles — what I optimise for
the rules I've
earned the
hard way.
  1. Clarity before speed. Most "execution problems" are clarity problems in disguise.
  2. Decisions before perfection. Decide at 70%, watch what breaks, fix fast.
  3. Reality over assumptions. Test the behaviour before building the thing.
  4. Follow-up over excitement. The boring 80% is where things actually get done.
  5. Knowing what to stop. Quitting the right thing on purpose is a skill, not a failure.
