How I got here

I grew up in Barh, near Patna in Bihar, and built the rest of myself in Kerala. Moving that far that young taught me the first operator's skill before I had a word for it: read a new system fast, figure out what actually runs it, then make yourself useful.

The pattern shows up everywhere I've worked. At VS-Kart I watched coordination break for want of a follow-up. With Jeevan Jhola I learned what it costs to ignore reality, and what it's worth to face it. At IIIT Kottayam I built something technical and learned to measure it honestly. At the Game Jam I ran a team under a 48-hour clock and shipped. Now, in a founder's office, I'm doing the same loop on a bigger surface — closer to where the real decisions get made.

I'm not calling myself a project manager yet; that's where I'm headed, and I'd rather earn the title than print it. What I am, today, is the person who turns an unclear idea into something that moves.

05

How I work

  1. 01Clarity before speed.Most execution problems are clarity problems wearing a costume. Name the real thing first and half the chaos disappears.
  2. 02The detail is the strategy.You can't manage from the top what you've never touched at the bottom. I'd rather know how the stock reconciles than have an opinion about it.
  3. 03Follow-up beats excitement.The person who closes the loop usually wins. It's unglamorous, which is exactly why it's rare.
  4. 04Test reality before building for it.People agree with a good idea and then do whatever's easiest — so I check what they actually do, not what they say, before committing resources.
  5. 05Knowing what to stop is a skill.Starting is easy. Quitting the right thing, on purpose, before it drains more than it's worth, is the harder and more useful decision.
